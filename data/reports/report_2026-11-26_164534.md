### TL;DR

The paper studies diffusion-based adversarial purification for vision-language agents operating on native-resolution multi-element inputs, showing that classifier-oriented purifier designs mislead agentic defense evaluation and design.

### Motivation

Content providers can perturb open-model vision-language agents via images or text strokes, and existing adversarial purifiers were designed and evaluated for fixed-resolution classifier/natural-image benchmarks rather than native-resolution agentic inputs.

### Method

The authors evaluate diffusion purifiers on an agent built with Qwen3-VL-8B under grey-box attacks at native resolution, compare against cheap input transformations at matched clean cost, and test on web-shop pages and a held-out set with a second VLM.

Resizing inputs to the diffusion model resolution severely increases clean TextVQA cost, while the Divide-and-Conquer purifier retains 85.0% grey-box robust accuracy on web-shop pages versus at most 40.0% for cheap transformations, with robustness attributable to global noise level.

### Conclusion

Agentic inputs should not be treated as classifier inputs, and the authors derive three defense requirements and release their evaluation protocol.
