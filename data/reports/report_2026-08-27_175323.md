- **Source**: arXiv  |  **Feed**: arXiv Computer Science
- **Direction**: General / Other
- **Published**: 2026-08-27
- **Authors**: Daniela P\"ohn Helin Omer
- **Link**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25670](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25670)
- **Tags**: `social engineering`  `phishing`  `vishing`  `smishing`  `psychological factors`

### TL;DR

This paper studies the effectiveness of different social engineering attack types (phishing, vishing, smishing, etc.) in leveraging psychological factors (authority, trust, greed, etc.) through a small laboratory study.

### Motivation

To understand which social engineering attack types are most effective for specific psychological factors and vice versa, to better defend against such attacks.

### Method

A laboratory study with 12 participants, each presented with all 25 combinations of 5 psychological factors and 5 social engineering attack types, measuring success rates.

### Result

Spear-phishing was most effective for authority, trust, and greed; the most successful combination was spear-phishing using greed; some combinations (pop-ups with authority, smishing with authority, vishing with curiosity) had no success.

### Conclusion

The findings suggest that certain psychological factors can be more effectively exploited by specific attack types, but the small sample size indicates the need for further research.

*Keywords: social engineering, phishing, vishing, smishing, psychological factors*

## 143. MeMark: Membrane-Space Watermarking For Spiking Neural Networks

- **Score**: 0.0 / 10  |  **Recommended**: ❌ No
- **Source**: arXiv  |  **Feed**: arXiv Computer Science
- **Direction**: General / Other
- **Published**: 2026-08-27
- **Authors**: Roberto Ria\~no, Gorka Abad, Stjepan Picek, Aitor Urbieta
- **Link**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25738](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25738)
- **Tags**: `tori`  `pruning`  `convolution`  `Spiking Neural Networks`  `Watermarking`

### TL;DR

The paper proposes MeMark, a watermarking technique for spiking neural networks that embeds a multi-bit identifier in the membrane state of selected LIF neurons to verify checkpoint reuse.

### Motivation

Existing SNN watermarks are output-based and can be easily removed by replacing the output head, so a more robust watermark is needed for the checkpoint-reuse setting.

### Method

MeMark embeds bits by driving selected Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (LIF) neurons to chosen firing thresholds; the verifier extracts bits using the same thresholds without a learned decoder.

### Result

MeMark is evaluated on multiple SNN architectures, including SpikeGPT; all 20 independent 64-bit keys pass verification, resist fine-tuning, pruning, quantization, and head replacement, and show resilience against adaptive attacks.

### Conclusion

MeMark provides strong ownership evidence for SNN checkpoints, remaining effective against common removal attacks and adversarial manipulations.

*Keywords: Spiking Neural Networks, Watermarking, Checkpoint verification, Membrane state, Leaky Integrate-and-Fire*

- **Direction**: General / Other
- **Published**: 2026-08-27
- **Authors**: Vishnu Bondalakunta, Arman Zareian Jahromi, Shuangqing Wei, George Amariucai
- **Link**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25750](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25750)
- **Tags**: `privacy`  `sat`  `face-swapping`  `privacy protection`  `identity leakage`

### TL;DR

The paper proposes a linear stochastic model to interpret and predict identity leakage in face-swapping anonymization, aiming for explainable privacy guarantees.

### Motivation

Empirical evaluations reveal significant target identity leakage in face-swapping anonymization, prompting a need to understand and predict this leakage.

### Method

A linear stochastic model is developed that treats face-swappers as transformations on identity embeddings, fitted to empirical observations to derive testable predictions.

### Result

The model offers an interpretable account of the leakage mechanism and enables prediction of leakage, grounding privacy assessments in principled analysis.

### Conclusion

The work aims to make formal privacy guarantees explainable and perfectible rather than purely observational.

*Keywords: face-swapping, privacy protection, identity leakage, interpretable model, linear stochastic model*

## 145. Rethinking The Foundations Of Two-Sided Ai Models For 6g

- **Score**: 0.0 / 10  |  **Recommended**: ❌ No
- **Source**: arXiv  |  **Feed**: arXiv Computer Science
- **Direction**: General / Other
- **Published**: 2026-08-27
- **Authors**: Yongjeong Oh, Zihan Chen, Timothy J. O'shea, Junyong Shin, Jinho Choi, Yo-Seb Jeon, Jihong Park
- **Link**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22918](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22918)
- **Tags**: `defi`  `two-sided AI models`  `6G`  `channel feedback`  `zeroth-order fine-tuning`

### TL;DR

This paper revisits practical assumptions for two-sided AI models in 6G and proposes solutions for legacy coexistence, channel adaptation, and multi-vendor fine-tuning.

### Motivation

Existing two-sided AI models for 6G rely on isolation from legacy users, predefined channel conditions, and gradient-based fine-tuning, which hinder practical deployment.

### Method

The authors integrate two-sided processing into the 5G NR protocol stack, construct a compact model table with surrogate channels for adaptation, and employ zeroth-order fine-tuning with scalar feedback.

### Result

Validation on a real-world testbed shows compatibility with NR and improvements in task performance, training/storage overhead, and interoperability.

### Conclusion

These approaches advance practical deployment of two-sided AI models for 6G, highlighting open challenges.

*Keywords: two-sided AI models, 6G, channel feedback, zeroth-order fine-tuning, 5G NR coexistence*
