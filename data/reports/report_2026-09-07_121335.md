- **Score**: 0.0 / 10  |  **Recommended**: ❌ No
- **Source**: arXiv  |  **Feed**: arXiv Computer Science
- **Direction**: General / Other
- **Published**: 2026-09-07
- **Authors**: Chang Liu, Edward Raff, Kristopher Micinski
- **Link**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05370](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05370)
- **Tags**: `cve`  `Decompilation`  `LLM`  `Behavioral divergence`  `Recompilability`

### TL;DR

This paper proposes Decompile-Diverge, a behavioral comparison oracle for evaluating LLM-based decompilers, showing that recompilability metrics can miss behavioral divergences and discovered vulnerabilities vanishing.

### Motivation

Traditional decompilers leave placeholders, while LLM-based decompilers are judged by recompilability and test passing, which can reward the wrong path and miss divergences on other inputs or loss of vulnerabilities.

### Method

Decompile-Diverge synthesizes a driver for each function, grows a fuzzing corpus from the reference, and reruns decompiled code on the same inputs to detect behavioral changes.

### Result

Across eight systems in nine configurations, candidates passing shipped tests still diverge on 4.9% overall (up to 13% for a single system); on real GitHub functions and CVE-grounded functions, recompilability and behavioral agreement can conflict, and up to 10% of vulnerabilities exhibit Crash Absence.

### Conclusion

The paper highlights the inadequacy of existing evaluation metrics for LLM decompilers and proposes a method to detect behavioral divergence, urging more robust evaluation.

*Keywords: Decompilation, LLM, Behavioral divergence, Recompilability, Fuzzing*

## 143. Ber-pef: Unified Human Mobility Predictability Evaluation Via Bayes Error Rate Estimation

- **Score**: 0.0 / 10  |  **Recommended**: ❌ No
- **Source**: arXiv  |  **Feed**: arXiv Computer Science
- **Direction**: General / Other
- **Published**: 2026-09-07
- **Authors**: En Xu, Jingtao Ding, Zhiwen Yu, Yong Li
- **Link**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04292](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04292)
- **Tags**: `prediction`  `human mobility`  `predictability`  `Bayes error rate`  `evaluation framework`

### TL;DR

This paper introduces a framework for evaluating human mobility predictability estimators using Bayes error rate estimation without observable ground truth.

### Motivation

The ground truth for human mobility predictability is not directly observable, making it difficult to compare and evaluate different estimators on real data.

### Method

The framework maps symbolic sequences, numeric trajectories, contextual features, and learned representations into a common feature-label space, then evaluates estimators along controlled perturbation curves against a shared predictability reference interval.

### Result

Experiments on several datasets (Foursquare NYC/TKY, GeoLife, T-Drive) show that BER-based estimators achieve lower reference discrepancy than existing methods, and the protocol supports unified evaluation across heterogeneous inputs.

### Conclusion

The proposed framework offers a unified and verifiable method for comparing predictability estimators on heterogeneous mobility data when ground-truth is unavailable.

*Keywords: human mobility, predictability, Bayes error rate, evaluation framework, perturbation analysis*
