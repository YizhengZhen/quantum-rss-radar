- **Tags**: `Gaussian states`  `genuine multipartite entanglement`  `full inseparability`  `separability`  `covariance matrix`

### TL;DR

The paper proves that for finite-mode Gaussian states, full inseparability (entanglement across every bipartition) coincides with genuine multipartite entanglement, removing the convex mixing ambiguity present for general mixed states.

### Motivation

For general mixed states, entanglement across all bipartitions does not imply genuine multipartite entanglement because biseparable decompositions may switch the separable cut from term to term. Whether this ambiguity persists for Gaussian states was an open question.

### Method

The proof combines a spectral selector with a holomorphic rigidity argument that converts one product vector in the square-root range of a Gaussian state into a block-local covariance certificate. Only the target state is required to be Gaussian; the decomposition may be continuous and contain arbitrary non-Gaussian states.

### Result

It is shown that any Gaussian density operator in the trace-norm-closed convex class generated by states separable across a finite family of partitions is already separable across one fixed partition in the family. Consequently, full inseparability and GME coincide, Gaussian k-separability and k-producibility reduce to fixed-partition tests, and party-wise tensor powers cannot activate GME from a biseparable Gaussian state.

### Conclusion

Partition mixing, a generic mixed-state mechanism, adds no new exact finite-mode Gaussian states. This simplifies the characterization of multipartite entanglement in continuous-variable Gaussian systems and tightens the connection between full inseparability and genuine multipartite entanglement.

*Keywords: Gaussian states, genuine multipartite entanglement, full inseparability, separability, covariance matrix*
