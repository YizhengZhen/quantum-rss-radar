### TL;DR

The paper demonstrates that the quantum Markov length, a length scale governing conditional mutual information decay in many-body states, is unstable under weak local channels, challenging its use for defining mixed-state phases.

### Motivation

The Markov length has been proposed as a tool to characterize mixed-state phases of matter, but its stability under local perturbations—a prerequisite for a meaningful order parameter—was not established.

### Method

The authors construct explicit examples in a one-dimensional fermionic chain using a fine-tuned depth-two circuit of nearest-neighbor Gaussian channels that maps the vacuum to a state with algebraically decaying conditional mutual information, and they provide an analogous construction on a qubit chain. ### Conclusion

The results show that the Markov length is not a stable characteristic of mixed-state phases under weak local channels, posing a challenge to recent definitions of mixed-state phase equivalence based on this quantity.
