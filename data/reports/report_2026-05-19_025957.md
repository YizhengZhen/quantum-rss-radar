### TL;DR

This paper proves that the Gallai Vertex Problem is complete for the complexity class Θ₂ᵖ, settling its computational complexity and implying inapproximability results for longest path transversal number.

### Motivation

The Gallai Vertex Problem (whether a graph has a vertex contained in all its longest paths) had unknown complexity for decades; the authors aimed to determine its exact computational complexity.

### Method

The authors use polynomial-time reductions from a known Θ₂ᵖ-complete problem (e.g., the critical satisfiability problem) to the Gallai Vertex Problem, and also prove hardness of approximation via gap reductions.

### Result

The problem is Θ₂ᵖ-complete, which is a complexity class above NP and below Σ₂ᵖ in the polynomial hierarchy. Consequently, the longest path transversal number cannot be approximated within a factor better than 2 unless P=NP, and for any constant k, approximation within factor k is also hard.

### Conclusion

This completely resolves the complexity of the Gallai Vertex Problem and provides strong inapproximability results for related transversal problems.
