### TL;DR

The paper studies a partially adaptive multistage stochastic programming formulation for optimizing revision timing and committed layout sequences in matrix assembly layouts under demand uncertainty.

### Motivation

Manufacturing systems with high product variety and volatile demand can benefit from reconfigurable matrix assembly layouts, but existing models restrict redesign to annual revisions and ignore the trade-off between flexibility and advance commitment.

### Method

The authors introduce the layout revision timing problem, formulate it as a partially adaptive multistage stochastic program, and develop a cost-based extension, a scenario-adaptive extension, an exact dynamic program, and an integer L-shaped method with monotonicity-based and decomposition-enhanced cuts.

### Result

On automotive instances with scenario trees approaching 10,000 nodes, the combined model saves on average 4.99% in expected station-use cost versus full adaptivity and 28.65% versus full commitment at the base-case discount.

### Conclusion

The work provides optimization methods and managerial insight into when finer revision calendars and partial commitment are economically valuable, especially when demand paths diverge and discounts justify commitment selectively.
