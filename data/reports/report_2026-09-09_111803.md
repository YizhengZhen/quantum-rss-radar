- **Source**: arXiv  |  **Feed**: arXiv Computer Science
- **Direction**: General / Other
- **Published**: 2026-09-09
- **Authors**: Haris Aziz
- **Link**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06282](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06282)
- **Tags**: `fair allocation`  `indivisible goods`  `additive valuations`  `pairwise maximin share fairness`  `PMMS`

### TL;DR

This paper resolves an open question by constructing a counterexample showing that pairwise maximin share (PMMS) allocations need not exist for strictly positive additive valuations with four agents.

### Motivation

The existence of PMMS allocations for positive additive valuations was an open problem in fair division; this paper aims to settle it.

### Method

The author constructs a specific instance with four agents and strictly positive additive valuations and analyzes whether a complete PMMS allocation exists.

### Result

The constructed instance admits no complete PMMS allocation, providing a counterexample.

### Conclusion

PMMS allocations do not always exist for positive additive valuations, closing the open question negatively.

*Keywords: fair allocation, indivisible goods, additive valuations, pairwise maximin share fairness, PMMS*

- **Tags**: `cooperative MSR codes`  `optimal-access`  `sub-packetization`  `parity-check matrix`  `MDS codes`

### TL;DR

This paper presents new constructions of optimal-access cooperative MSR codes with reduced sub-packetization, using parity-check matrices and a unified transformation framework.

### Motivation

To reduce the sub-packetization level of optimal-access cooperative MSR codes, which is critical for practical distributed storage systems with limited disk IO.

### Method

Proposes a direct explicit construction via designing parity-check matrices using building blocks, and a generic transformation framework that derives cooperative MSR codes from any MDS scalar code.

### Result

Both methods yield codes with sub-packetization reduced by a fraction compared to state-of-the-art, and previous constructions are shown to be special cases.

### Conclusion

The new constructions achieve lower sub-packetization while maintaining optimal-access properties, advancing the efficiency of distributed storage codes.

*Keywords: cooperative MSR codes, optimal-access, sub-packetization, parity-check matrix, MDS codes*

- **Direction**: General / Other
- **Published**: 2026-09-09
- **Authors**: Liwen Jiang, Xu Shi, Rufeng Xiao, Changhao Yan, Rujun Jiang, Zhiang Wang, Keren Zhu
- **Link**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06408](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06408)
- **Tags**: `optimization`  `benchmark`  `equilibrium`  `3D-IC`  `tier assignment`

### TL;DR

This paper presents a differentiable partitioning framework for tier assignment in 3D-ICs that integrates placement and hybrid bonding terminal awareness to optimize wirelength.

### Motivation

Hybrid bonding-enabled 3D-ICs offer benefits but tier assignment is challenging due to its joint impact on wirelength and terminal allocation.

### Method

The approach relaxes discrete tier assignment to continuous probabilities, uses a Dual-Max 3D wirelength model to capture per-tier HPWL, adds a terminal-aware cutsize penalty, and enforces a balance constraint.

### Result

Experiments on OpenROAD benchmarks show reductions in D2D HPWL by 2.0% over min-cut baselines and 12.1% over a state-of-the-art 3D placer.

### Conclusion

The proposed method effectively improves 3D placement quality and the code is open-sourced for reproducibility.

*Keywords: 3D-IC, tier assignment, differentiable partitioning, hybrid bonding, wirelength optimization*

## 145. Watch And Crack: Password Inference From Smart-Glasses Video

- **Score**: 0.0 / 10  |  **Recommended**: ❌ No
- **Source**: arXiv  |  **Feed**: arXiv Computer Science
- **Direction**: General / Other
- **Published**: 2026-09-09
- **Authors**: Avishai Wool Yoav Orenbach
- **Link**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06539](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06539)
- **Tags**: `rank`  `side-channel attack`  `keystroke inference`  `smart glasses`  `smartphone security`

### TL;DR

This paper presents a video-based side-channel attack using smart glasses to infer passwords typed on smartphones, leveraging fingertip tracking and neural networks.

### Motivation

To demonstrate a realistic and generalized attack on alphanumeric passwords typed on smartphones in public, using accessible smart glasses.

### Method

The pipeline tracks device and fingertips from video, predicts keystrokes with a self-supervised ensemble of neural networks, estimates keyboard layout, and combines video-derived probabilities with password statistics from leaked passwords.

### Result

Human-chosen passwords up to 16 characters can be cracked in hours to days, and random passwords up to 13 characters in under an hour, reducing entropy by up to 60 bits.

### Conclusion

The attack is effective and generalizes across devices and poses, highlighting a significant security threat from wearable cameras.

*Keywords: side-channel attack, keystroke inference, smart glasses, smartphone security, video surveillance*
